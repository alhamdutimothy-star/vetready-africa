Status: implemented Phase 3J software; no professional governance is seeded.

The protected Phase 3I checkpoint is `3f3972d981a85d640285d0a4efd2d9a65ee91990`, tagged `phase-3i-audited-checkpoint`. Authentication and migrations 001–017 are protected. Phase 3F remains a demonstration workflow: its practical flags, decisions, receipts and historical meanings do not change.

## Reuse and separation

New submissions use existing `evidence_submissions`, immutable submitted `evidence_submission_versions`, exact `evidence_submission_competencies` and canonical `competency_evidence` receipts. A new case references one exact receipt and rubric version. It does not create a second competency, evidence, training, readiness or general audit ledger. The original receipt remains UNVERIFIED; an append-only decision overlay records what subsequently happened to that evidence. Existing Phase 3F receipts are never relabelled.

Versioned rubrics pin pathway, child, method, facet, structured criteria and criterion versions, evidence requirements, allowed verification processes, policy/approval references and an explicitly configured moderation requirement. No pass mark, weight, expiry duration or veterinary criterion is supplied. PROPOSED rubrics are demo-only. APPROVED rubrics require independently attributed approval and an approved child definition. Both are immutable.

Professional eligibility is a separate exact-rubric assessor authorization, with an external approval reference and explicit validity timestamp. Database capabilities alone do not qualify an assessor. `verification:govern` is a new separately provisioned capability; its role has no assigned users. ADMIN and SUPER_ADMIN do not inherit it. Governance records can only be provisioned by a trusted operator acting under this separate capability; there is no public or administrator shortcut approval endpoint. External eligibility, qualification and rubric-approval procedures still require domain-expert governance. Deployments must not treat an arbitrary reference string as evidence that real-world approval occurred.

## Review lifecycle

No event means PENDING_REVIEW/unassigned. ASSIGN records initial assignment or reassignment; START_REVIEW records work beginning; RECUSE records a declared category and reason and permanently excludes that assessor from deciding this case, including after attempted reassignment. Assignment operators see case references and assignment history, not raw evidence or private findings. Only the currently assigned independent assessor sees the review account and structured findings. A new assignment removes the previous assessor's access. Revoked/expired professional authorization or a revoked rubric also removes private assessor reads and queue visibility; an assignment is not durable authority.

DECISION stores VERIFIED, PARTIALLY_DEMONSTRATED, MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED, NOT_DEMONSTRATED or INVALIDATED. Findings refer to exact criterion/version and the case's exact evidence receipt. The human chooses an explicit outcome; software never computes a professional pass from findings or learning. A correction links the latest decision through `supersedes_decision_id`; no decision is overwritten. The database derives and stores before/after evidence maturity on each decision; correction and invalidation history retains those transitions. INVALIDATED requires a predecessor. A separately assigned moderator can make a MODERATION decision with the separate moderation capability and cannot moderate their own preceding decision. Where the policy requires moderation, a primary VERIFIED decision remains ineffective until that independent moderation decision exists. No universal moderation requirement is invented.

Candidate WITHDRAW is available before a decision and stops further case activity. APPEAL records a request without rewriting or invalidating the original decision. Appeals administration, resolution procedures and legal timelines are deferred. Reassessment creates a new case and new submitted evidence referencing a same-owner, same-child/pathway/method predecessor with a decision. Only the latest case in that lineage contributes to the current verification projection; predecessor chronology remains readable. A superseded case cannot receive further review decisions; immutable owner appeals and a deciding reviewer's own recusal remain possible. A withdrawn pending reassessment with a predecessor may receive one same-scope successor; predecessor identities and prior outcomes are not rewritten.

## Projection and historical compatibility

Live owner competency-profile and Skills Passport responses add `governedVerification`, a sanitised projection containing case/evidence, exact competency/pathway/rubric/policy references, process/time, decision/time, candidate-safe explanation and explicit before/after evidence levels. It excludes raw evidence, assessor identifiers, observations, private rationale, conflicts and appeal reasons. The Passport is still private; no employer payload is expanded.

The projection requires non-demo approved governance, current validity of the exact authorization used for the decision, an active assessor, nonrevoked rubric, approved child, active source and any configured moderation. Revocation/expiry/invalidation cannot leave an effective positive claim. It is separate from engine-1 facets and immutable snapshots; historical engine replay and Phase 3I plan digests remain unchanged. Learning-only evidence remains learning-only. This phase does not retrospectively recompute engine-1 gap receipts or close unconfigured professional requirements.

The exact-authorization check applies to every contributing PRIMARY/MODERATION decision, following moderation predecessors until the relevant PRIMARY. Each recorded authorization must match that decision's actor and rubric, remain unexpired and nonrevoked, and retain the existing active review capability. The same database function guards new VERIFIED moderation and live projection. A later authorization grant cannot revive an older revoked decision; a valid independent new PRIMARY starts a new supporting chain and still requires any configured moderation. Concurrent revocation may preserve an immutable decision recorded during the race, but a subsequent current projection must withhold effectiveness once the supporting revocation commits. No historical decision or authorization is rewritten.

## Integrity and operations

Candidate identity and acting reviewer derive from authenticated server-side context. All API bodies are strict. Cases serialize event commands by row lock, expected revision and unique case/revision. Owner/command and actor/command uniqueness plus canonical input digests distinguish exact retries from changed command meanings. Stable owner locks serialize new submissions. Audit triggers run atomically and record references rather than private bodies. Failed canonical audit writes roll back the action. New tables are append-only.

Migration 018 is forward-only. Repeatability follows the existing checksummed migration ledger, not blind re-execution of DDL. Fresh and populated-017 upgrade checks are required. Shared database service credentials remain trusted infrastructure; this is not end-user RLS. Production needs least-privilege database roles, protected approval provisioning and governance operating procedures.

Evidence is text/metadata only. No upload adapter, public URL, private-storage claim, offline queue or veterinary standard is introduced. Private object storage/quarantine/scanning, farm-data consent/minimisation, retention/erasure policy, accessibility/load/live-provider validation and production operations remain launch blockers. Job Ready/Field Ready, certification, licensing and professional matching stay UNCONFIGURED.

## Independent-audit remediation

Current effective verification validates the canonical criterion contract and the selected decision's supporting review chain. MODERATION follows its predecessor reviews back through the relevant PRIMARY; recusal of any supporting actor withholds the claim. An independent new PRIMARY review starts a new support chain while retaining all preceding decisions/conflicts. Private reads and decisions remain unavailable to former/recused reviewers; the existing RECUSE event permits a former deciding reviewer to report their own conflict without receiving case content.

SQL and repository criterion validation now agree on exact JSON fields/types, UUIDs, positive safe-integer versions, unique criterion identities, 1–100 criteria and trimmed 1–1000 Unicode code-point text (the installed Zod semantics). Direct SQL validation and read/projection checks fail closed; these are technical representation constraints, not professional standards. Withdrawn reassessment recovery preserves the single-successor chain and does not automatically restore an earlier claim. Historical appeals do not change outcomes by themselves.
